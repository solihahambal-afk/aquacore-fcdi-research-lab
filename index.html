A fish-gill result is not guaranteed to improve. The model solves current, feed concentration along 60 segments, and a laminar pressure-drop estimate. It is a screening tool, not CFD or a validated electrochemical simulation.

Model mechanisms and trade-offs

Electrical conductivity is estimated from NaCl-equivalent concentration; resistance combines solution, membrane and slurry/contact contributions. The model integrates local salt transfer in 60 axial control volumes using Faraday's law with a specified charge efficiency. A simplified laminar parallel-plate hydraulic relation estimates pressure losses. Branching may shorten flow paths, but narrower gaps increase pressure drop. The model does not independently resolve 3D flow maldistribution, boundary-layer transport, electrode polarization, regeneration kinetics or slurry clogging.

Published comparison — not AquaCore data

A 2026 study reported approximately 33% higher average salt removal rate and 15% higher energy-normalized salt removal for its own experimentally tested streamlined FCDI channel relative to its control. This is evidence that geometry can matter, not a prediction for our gill design.

Do not claim the model's numerical outputs as verified prototype performance. Real FCDI performance requires calibration with current–voltage curves, membrane resistance, slurry properties and measured concentration profiles.

Reproducible methods & scientific references

This browser model uses explicitly adjustable parameters, discretized salt balance and a simple hydraulic resistance approximation. Geometry optimization enumerates candidate gap/branch combinations to maximize salt removed per electrical-plus-pump energy, with a minimum 95% of the baseline salt transfer. It can legitimately return a worse gill result. Numerical predictions are conditional on assumed material properties; the scientific literature below supplies motivation, not AquaCore calibration.

Mishra et al., Desalination (2026), validated streamlined FCDI geometry · Shi et al., Water Research (2023), FCDI energy model · Tubular FCDI and CFD ion transport (2021).
